### SURF — surface-spec claims and coupling
Hunt the claims a UI/UX spec makes that nothing in the changeset can check: an as-built statement with no source of truth behind it, a claim sourced to another document where the project's answer says code or shipped configuration, intent written in the as-built voice or an as-built record whose mode word still says draft, a tier-2 section present in a design-intent spec or missing from an as-built one, an adjacent surface absorbed into this spec instead of named beside it, a rationale that describes the surface rather than saying why it exists, a visual-language constraint superseded without the spec saying that it supersedes it or why, a platform-specific statement written as though it were universal — a placement frame, a device name or an input row assuming a modality the project's binding does not target — a state dependency the behaviour section relies on that nothing in the changeset exposes, an open question the spec resolved on its own authority rather than recording for the ruling, a spec checked in apart from the change it describes, and a ruling that is undated, attributed to a person rather than a role, or written somewhere the project's binding does not put rulings.
Do NOT report: the three structural properties `ui-spec-complete` owns — a missing or empty frontmatter key, an absent closing open-questions section, and a cited link target that does not resolve — those are this pack's gate check, not a review finding; nor any judgment about whether the design is good, which is the human's ruling and not a reviewer's: whether a colour scheme reads at a glance, whether a visual reference is honoured, and whether a pillar was softened are the three the human takes at the ruling stop.
