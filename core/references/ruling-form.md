# Ruling form

How the PM puts a ruling to the human at a hard stop. Every skill whose stop asks the human to rule, agree or give their word cites this file at that step.

1. **In the session, in plain language.** The PM's own message states the issue — what is being ruled on, and why it is up for ruling now — in plain language. A pointer to a file, a brief, an audit or a collation is never the whole of it: files carry the detail and the evidence; the message carries the decision.
2. **Each option with its pros and cons.** Where the ruling chooses among options, each option is named with what it gives and what it costs. The PM's recommendation, where it has one, is marked and comes first. Where the ruling is a yes or a no on a gate, the message says what a yes authorizes and what stays open or undone either way.
3. **Several rulings at one stop** — for example one per review finding — are each put this way, briefly, in the stop's own order.
